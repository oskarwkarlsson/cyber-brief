N-able takes hosted N-central offline worldwide after a researcher publishes 30+ zero-days with exploits

Researcher Sean Bales released a GitHub repo called Able2Shell late Sunday with proof-of-concept exploits for more than 30 alleged flaws in N-central, the remote monitoring and management platform MSPs use to run customer fleets. The worst chain is an unauthenticated XXE in service import that leads to password reset, a rogue Default Administrator account and command execution on the server. N-able confirmed a critical incident, took hosted N-central down across the Americas, Europe and APAC, told on-prem customers to take servers offline, and is building a hotfix; it says it has seen no exploitation so far. Bales says discovery was AI-assisted and he skipped coordinated disclosure because earlier reports went unpatched. Takeaway: with public exploits out, any exposed on-prem N-central should be offline now, and RMM compromise means compromise of every managed customer.

France attributes the October fridge sabotage to IRGC unit Shahid Kaveh and seeks arrest warrants

France's Cyber Crisis Coordination Centre (ANSSI, cyber command, DGSE, DGSI) says the IRGC's Shahid Kaveh unit, previously tied to attacks on US water utility controllers, sabotaged refrigeration systems at about 700 French grocery stores in October, causing large food losses and at least 15 fires. ANSSI also saw the group hitting Schneider Electric equipment at several small hydro plants, with moderate disruption. Paris prosecutors opened a judicial investigation into the unit's head and two deputies and requested international arrest warrants; France summoned Iran's ambassador and will push EU sanctions. The report does not cover the second wave in early November across at least 11 European countries. Takeaway: this is an official state attribution for destructive OT attacks on retail, so internet-exposed building and refrigeration controllers are a live target in Europe.

IBM Aspera Faspex authorization bypass added to CISA KEV, due 12 November

CISA added CVE-2026-123218 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list on 9 November: an authenticated user can craft a message to bypass authorization and escalate privileges in IBM Aspera Faspex, the file-transfer server. Due date is 12 November with forensic triage required; ransomware use is listed as unknown. It landed after our 18:30 check. Takeaway: Faspex has been a ransomware favourite before, so patch per IBM's bulletin and review accounts for unexpected privilege changes.

Japanese mailer ARARA breach exposes 77 million addresses across ~380 clients, SEGA counts 2.2 million

SEGA disclosed that a breach at its newsletter provider ARARA exposed 2.2 million subscriber email addresses plus 92,300 records with other data still being identified. ARARA says the wider incident hit 77 million addresses from nearly 380 corporate clients, including MOS Burger (7.5 million) and Shochiku Multiplex Theatres (3.3 million), through a web server vulnerability, and that the attackers used its service to send over 7 million emails impersonating credit card companies in late October. Takeaway: expect convincing phishing to Japanese consumer lists sent from legitimate brand mailers.
