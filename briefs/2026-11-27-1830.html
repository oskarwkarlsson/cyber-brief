US Air Force Cloud One reportedly hacked

Reuters reports that hackers breached Cloud One, the Air Force's enterprise cloud that hosts DoD applications for operations, personnel, logistics and maintenance. The intrusion was found within the past month during a platform upgrade and disrupted app migrations and software updates. One source says an unidentified software vulnerability was used; US Cyber Command is assessing impact. Attacker, scope and any data theft are unknown, and the Air Force declined to comment. Single outlet, anonymous sources. Takeaway: the latest in a run of federal breaches (FBI, CIA, Treasury's FinCEN); watch for a named product or CVE.

AFP Forum offline after cyberattack

Agence France-Presse confirmed in an internal email, quoted in its own reporting, that AFP Forum, the platform clients use to browse and download its stories, photos and video, was hit overnight on Thursday. AFP took it offline as a precaution, is investigating with outside experts and is offering subscribers other ways to get content. It remained down on Friday. No attacker, data impact or restore timeline disclosed. Takeaway: newsrooms relying on AFP Forum should expect delays and treat any "AFP" login or content emails with caution while it is down.

WeaveLotus: suspected Vietnam-linked South China Sea espionage

SOCRadar describes a previously undocumented cluster it calls WeaveLotus (campaign "RedOps", August to September 2026) with five confirmed intrusions at military, government and legislative bodies in Thailand and the Philippines and reconnaissance against Indonesian agencies. Initial access came via web app flaws and default credentials in GeoServer, JasperReports, osTicket and exposed Laravel debug mode, then reverse SSH tunnels and lateral movement. Attribution is moderate-confidence (Vietnamese code comments, UTC+7 working hours, targeting). Takeaway: GeoServer keeps showing up as an entry point; patch and lock down default creds.

Microsoft Store app delivers Vinci RAT

eSentire found a fake Snipping Tool site (snipp.codes-team[.]com) serving a Microsoft-signed installer that pulled "Setup Assistant", an obfuscated Electron app published on the Microsoft Store by ETPS Ltd. The app acted as a backdoor (C2 setup-assistant[.]com) running remote PowerShell and JavaScript, which disabled AMSI, set a scheduled task every 10 minutes and installed a previously undocumented PowerShell RAT, Vinci RAT (C2 monkeysholder[.]info). Sibling subdomains spoof Teams and Roblox downloads. The app is no longer on the Store. Takeaway: Store listings are not a trust signal; hunt for Setup Assistant and the C2 domains.
