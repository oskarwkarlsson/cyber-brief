Vulns

N-central "4EverAble2Shell" moves from probing to compromise: 21 production servers mining Monero, 19 of them on Hotfix 2

The public PoC dropped at 03:58 UTC on 12 Nov; Huntback saw full exploit-chain attempts from 157.20.241[.]16 from 09:16 UTC (Apache Axis CVE-2026-90436 for code execution, then a root escalation via nable_wrapper.pl) installing an XMRig miner. The miner's public pool listed 21 live rigs named after victim IPs, all serving N-central login pages, 19 running 2026.4.1.26 (Hotfix 2, released 10 Nov); one is in Sweden. Previdian independently saw full-chain attempts on its honeypot. Exploitation requires the Legacy API to be enabled. N-able says it sees scanning but no compromise of N-central hosted servers. Takeaway: on-prem N-central owners should disable the Legacy API now, keep servers off the internet, and treat any hit as root on a box that manages customer endpoints, not a miner cleanup.

Crypto

DOJ moves to seize $1.2 billion stolen from Binance and disrupts North Korea's "FaceStealer" fake meeting campaign

The Justice Department got court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the $6.3 billion Binance theft it attributes, with the FBI, to TraderTraitor; about $2.7 billion was frozen in total. Separately, the FBI disrupted cloud infrastructure behind FaceStealer, which it says stole over $400 million in 18 months: Calendly invites from hijacked accounts lead to fake Zoom or Teams pages, pre-recorded videos of past victims (and sometimes deepfakes) fake a full meeting, then a "fix" for an audio error installs a cookie stealer, keylogger and backdoor. The FBI seized 900+ victim webcam videos, ties FaceStealer to APT38, and says it targets crypto staff and npm and Rust crate maintainers. It is unclear whether FaceStealer was used against Binance. Takeaway: treat any "install this to fix your audio" prompt in a meeting as an attack, even from a known contact.

Actors

Luna Moth hits law firms a second time, now stealing Microsoft Graph tokens through Copilot with no malware

ReliaQuest says the extortion group is retargeting firms that likely refused its first demand. Victims get a broken "business report" PDF, then a call from fake IT support offering to fix it; on a screen share they are walked into opening Copilot in the browser, opening developer tools, copying the Graph access token and pasting it into a public text site. The attacker then downloads files via the Graph API. MFA is satisfied because the user completes a legitimate flow. Takeaway: enforce Continuous Access Evaluation, block developer tools on managed browsers, and tell staff IT will never ask for tokens.
