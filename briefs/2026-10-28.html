Incidents

Japan's GMO says the Onamae Desktop Cloud attacker leaked company and customer data after a ¥8 billion ransom demand

GMO Internet detected the intrusion on Oct 20 and says malware may have reached up to 26,739 customer servers on the VPS service, which is popular with MT4/MT5 traders. It refused a ¥8 billion (about $50M) demand, warned on Oct 26 that the attacker planned to leak data and trigger ransomware planted on customer servers, and on Oct 27 told customers to back up, disconnect and power down their PCs before noon. GMO then confirmed data had been published (authenticity still being checked); no ransomware had fired by BleepingComputer's report. TechCrunch notes reports of unauthorized operations on trading accounts. GMO has not linked this to the ARTEX AI pentest tool it reported last week.

Vulns

CISA KEV adds Adobe Commerce/Magento CVE-2026-48356 and Brotli HTTP2Shell CVE-2026-111497, both due Nov 10

The Adobe Commerce and Magento bug is an unrestricted file upload that can lead to code execution. The Brotli listing means CISA now has evidence the HTTP2Shell decoder bug is exploited, a step up from the PoC and emergency-patch stage of Oct 26. Brotli is bundled in proxies, runtimes and language packages, so check vendored copies, not only the system library. The two PHP2Shell KEV entries from Oct 26 hit their federal deadline tomorrow, Oct 29.

AI/Cloud

Warp discloses a third-party vendor breach that exposed AI agent logs and telemetry

CEO Zach Lloyd says Warp spotted active exploitation of a vendor in its infrastructure on Friday and estimates about 1% of users may be affected. Agent decision traces and telemetry were exposed; Warp has not said whether prompts, code or credentials were included. Lloyd attributes the attack to "an agent swarm" (Warp's own claim) and CrowdStrike is investigating. It follows last week's OpenCode key incident, another sign that AI dev tools are a prime target. Warp users should rotate any secrets that may have appeared in agent sessions.

Crypto

Keystone will destroy 18,835 hardware wallets after an attacker stole its device verification key

Keystone says an attacker exploited a flaw in a third-party monitoring tool on its website, escalated privileges and took customer data plus the key its devices use to prove they are genuine. That key could let someone build counterfeits that pass Keystone's online check. Recovery phrases and wallet keys were not involved, and no counterfeits have been reported. Devices received before Oct 20 that pass the check are fine; anything delivered after that with the old key should be confirmed with Keystone support before use.
