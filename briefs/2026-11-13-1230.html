SilentAir: state-level actor runs SS7 and Diameter interception from compromised telcos in at least six Asian countries

Stockholm-based telecom security firm Enea named SilentAir in a report published 11 Nov (missed until now). It has tracked the group since early 2025 and assesses with high confidence that it operates at state level; its working hours and holiday lulls match China, but Enea rates that link low confidence. From hijacked signalling equipment inside operators in at least six Asian countries, SilentAir sends forged UpdateLocation messages (and the Diameter equivalent) that reroute a target's calls and SMS to the attacker; Enea saw 2FA codes arrive right after diversion for some targets. It also harvests authentication vectors, tracks location and sends large volumes of silent SMS, mostly to Taiwan at night, apparently to check diversions are still in place. Targets span a dozen more countries and territories and include political and government VIPs. Takeaway: SMS one-time codes are not safe for high-value accounts; for anyone travelling in or dealing with Taiwan and the region, use app-based or hardware MFA.

LIMINAL PANDA's new GTPDoor version hides command-and-control in GTP-U user-data traffic across 5 to 10 operators

In the same report, Enea says LIMINAL PANDA, a known telco-focused actor, was active again in 2026 across multiple operators, with evidence of 5 to 10 infected operators and possibly dozens more affected (SC Media cites Asia, Africa and Latin America). A new GTPDoor backdoor variant uses GTP-U, the protocol that carries subscribers' roaming data, which makes its traffic far harder to spot than the older control-plane version. Enea saw hundreds of attempted covert communications and says links between LIMINAL PANDA and SilentAir are possible but unconfirmed. Takeaway: operators need correlated visibility across signalling, GTP and internal systems; this is a carrier problem rather than something end users can fix.
