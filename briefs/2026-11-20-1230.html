Incidents

USDA resets every database password after an intrusion

Nextgov/FCW and Government Executive, citing three people and an internal email, report that USDA's Cybersecurity and Privacy Operations Center "uncovered a security incident" disclosed internally on Monday, forcing a reset of all database passwords in all environments. That took many apps offline, including tools for farmer payments, loans and aid status, with intermittent outages expected through the week and some VPN problems. Who did it and whether data left is unknown; USDA has not commented publicly. Takeaway: a US federal agency incident with real service impact; watch for a CISA or USDA statement.

JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard confirm third-party incidents

After the N0n extortion group claimed customer data from all three on November 16, JPMorgan said vendors serving the bank were hit and attackers accessed marketing data that may include customer names and emails (it will offer credit monitoring where appropriate); Visa tied the claims to a third-party provider in Asia Pacific with no card data affected; Mastercard said a provider exposed a limited set of email addresses for one benefit program. Takeaway: no payment data or networks hit, but expect targeted phishing against these cardholders.

INC Ransom lists Boeing's BSAS and Eni (claim, unverified)

INC Ransom added Boeing Sikorsky Aircraft Support, a Boeing Global Services unit that supports US special operations aviation, claiming seven servers, 3.6M+ files and M365 mailboxes with a December 3 deadline, and Italian energy major Eni, claiming about 22.5 GB of contracts, financial and procurement files. Neither company has confirmed. Takeaway: claim, unverified; both are high-value defense and energy targets, so watch for statements.

FastUnlock: unpatched Windows lock-screen bypass

Researcher Nightmare Eclipse (MSNightmare) posted a technique that uses Narrator inside the "I forgot my PIN" recovery flow to reach Settings and run programs as the non-admin WsiAccount without signing in. ThreatLocker reproduced it. It needs physical access and a Microsoft-linked account, and works on TPM-only BitLocker PCs because the drive is already unlocked. No patch or CVE and no known in-the-wild use. Takeaway: for laptops at risk of theft, add a BitLocker startup PIN and hibernate or shut down instead of sleep.
