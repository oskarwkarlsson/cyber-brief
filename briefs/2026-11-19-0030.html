Vulns

CISA KEV: LiteLLM, REDCap, Apache Axis and Synology DSM

CISA added four actively exploited flaws on November 18: BerriAI LiteLLM CVE-2026-40217 (remote code execution via bytecode rewriting at /guardrails/test_custom_code, due December 2), Vanderbilt REDCap CVE-2026-90817 (unauthenticated code injection through an unintended controller route), Apache Axis CVE-2026-90436 (unauthenticated RCE via a crafted SOAP fault that loads an attacker-chosen class) and Synology DSM CVE-2024-10441 (remote code execution in the system plugin daemon). The last three are due November 21. Takeaway: LiteLLM proxies and REDCap research servers are often internet-facing; patch or pull them off the internet now.

Ukraine and FBI arrest four in $40.5m ransomware network

Ukrainian police, working with the FBI, ran 66 searches and detained four suspected key members of a group of at least 27 people tied to more than $40.5m in ransoms from at least 28 companies, part of a wider 60+ member network that includes ex-Conti members. Court records name Chaos as the ransomware and victims including Warren Oil, Newpark Resources and Quantum Health. Initial access came from fake IT support calls using a custom platform with AI-generated voice messages, VPN phishing pages, spoofed numbers and TeamViewer or AnyDesk. Takeaway: treat unsolicited "IT support" calls asking for MFA codes or remote tools as an attack.

Wellington IT breach hits Irish credit unions

Belfast-based Wellington IT, which serves about 50 credit unions (43 in Ireland), was hit between October 2 and 4, when an automated script made five million requests to abuse an authorization flaw, according to notices seen by the Irish Examiner. Minister of State Robert Troy confirmed the link to RTÉ and called the notification delay "not acceptable". Saved payee names, IBANs, BICs, account numbers and addresses may be exposed; passwords, PINs and cards were not. The firm says it lacks logs to say whose data was taken. Takeaway: expect targeted payment-fraud phishing against members.
