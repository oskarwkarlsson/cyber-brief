CISA KEV: Drupal, Optimizely, Idunno AntiCsrf

CISA added three exploited flaws on Nov 24, all due Nov 27 for federal agencies. Drupal core CVE-2026-132165 (anonymous install.php abuse to take over a site or run code) was patched Nov 19 with no attacks known then; it is now confirmed exploited. Optimizely Web Experimentation CVE-2026-132224 is the signup-flow flaw that let attackers impersonate customer users and change Project JavaScript, the root cause of the skimmer injections. Idunno AntiCsrf CVE-2026-131914 is the end-of-life ASP.NET library with no fix; CISA says stop using it.

Radware breach exposes customer cloud keys

In a root cause report seen by BleepingComputer (shared by an anonymous source), Radware says an internal Cloud WAF management API was reachable from the internet with no authentication and was accessed on Nov 19, ten hours before detection. Exposed: customer contact details and, for some customers, credentials for log export to AWS S3, Azure Blob Storage or SFTP. If you use Radware Cloud WAF or Bot, rotate those export keys and SAS tokens now and check storage access logs since Nov 19.

Unclassified CIA intelligence system breached

A person familiar with the incident told AP that an unclassified CIA system handling open-source intelligence (internet, commercial satellite and data broker material) was breached; no classified data was involved and the issue is resolved. The New York Times reported it first. What was taken and who did it are unknown. Anonymous sources only, not confirmed by the CIA. It follows the FBI's probe of an apparent NCIC breach.

myColorado app hacked, about 150,000 hit

Colorado's IT office says hackers breached the myColorado app (digital ID, document storage and state services, 2.1 million users), found on Nov 18. Data tied to nearly 150,000 people was likely stolen; the state has not said what kind. The app is still offline and residents are told to carry physical IDs for Thanksgiving travel. No attribution.
