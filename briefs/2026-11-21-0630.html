Incidents

FBI probes apparent intrusion into its NCIC crime database

CNN, citing two people briefed on the investigation, reports that the FBI is investigating an apparent hacker breach of the National Crime Information Center, the nationwide system federal, state and local law enforcement use for warrants, criminal histories and background checks. NBC News confirmed with its own two sources and Reuters relayed the CNN report. The intrusion was detected in recent days; scope, attacker and whether data was read, altered or stolen are not public, and the FBI has not commented. It is the second FBI system breach in recent months after September's ShinyHunters-linked job-portal hack. A former FBI official's worry: not just exposure, but whether records officers rely on could be tampered with. Anonymous sources only.

"RansomwareHelp" CTO indicted: the "decryption" was a paid ransom

A Rhode Island indictment charges Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, co-founder and CTO of Florida-based ransomwarehelp.com, with wire fraud. The firm told victims "Do not pay the ransom!" and claimed proprietary cryptography, but in an FBI sting (agents posing as both victim and ransomware gang) it allegedly bought the decryptor for about $6,000 and billed the fake victim $16,010. Krebs also reports a 2024 case where the firm demanded remote AnyDesk access, apparently deleted ransom notes so the victim could not contact LockBit directly, and quoted $438,880. It follows the MonsterCloud and Cybersteward prosecutions. Takeaway: vet any "we never pay" recovery vendor, and keep your own copy of the ransom note.
