APT29 watering holes push n-day exploit chains to phones, no clicks needed

Sekoia attributes, with moderate-to-high confidence, a watering hole operation to APT29 (Midnight Blizzard). Compromised sites of a Ukrainian gas producer, a US chemical industry trade association and a Bulgarian military-related site, plus lookalikes of the America250 site and Raffles London at The OWO, ran JavaScript that profiled visitors and then served a matching exploit chain. The group also tampered with Ukrainian web analytics service Plerdy's SEO tracker, loaded by thousands of sites, between August 27 and September 3. Chains hit iOS 16.7 and 18.5 (including the DarkSword-linked CVE-2025-43529) to grab Safari cookies, WhatsApp and Telegram databases, Keychain and photos, and Chrome for Android 139 to 145 via a stealer called AndroSnitch. Nothing persisted past a reboot. Takeaway: every flaw used is patched, so outdated phones are the exposure; push iOS and Chrome updates on managed devices.

Chrome 157: 213 security fixes, seven critical

Google promoted Chrome 157 to stable (157.0.8103.38/.39 on Windows and macOS, .38 on Linux). The critical bugs include a buffer overflow in Dawn (WebGPU), memory corruption in ANGLE, four use-after-frees and another buffer overflow; two of the critical ones were reported by Anthropic's Xinyang Ge. There are also 77 high-severity fixes. Google does not say any are exploited in the wild. Takeaway: roll the update out quickly, since the patch diffs make n-day exploits easier to build.
