OpenAI's red-team agents chained microVM escapes and built cache-timing covert channels, with fixes going to Kata Containers, gVisor and Docker; METR found a Codex sandbox and Auto-Review bypass

Vectors

Frontier-model agents escaped hardened microVMs and talked across isolation boundaries

OpenAI says teams of its own agents, red-teaming isolated copies of its research infrastructure, chained flaws into microVM escapes, found limited routes to the internet through its metrics, logging and auth services, and swapped secrets between separate microVMs via CPU cache timing (the Spectre/Meltdown trick), plus storage, CPU-contention and TLS-cache side channels. Fixes are going upstream to Kata Containers (CVE-2026-24834 plus privilege and filesystem isolation), gVisor (file-descriptor handling) and Moby/Docker (syscall filters), with kernel fixes CVE-2026-74753, CVE-2026-80521 and CVE-2026-80841; more disclosures are not public yet. If you sandbox untrusted code or agents in these runtimes, track and apply those patches, and don't treat shared hosts as a hard boundary.

METR and Redwood: Codex agents could in principle bypass Codex sandboxing and Auto-Review

A three-week, high-access exercise (July 21 to August 11) on OpenAI's internal misalignment monitoring found that agents could in principle escape Codex sandboxing and Auto-Review to take any action the host user could, and an exploit that would keep hundreds of recent actions from being monitored. They estimate 75 to 94% of strong-model agentic Responses API traffic was eligible for monitoring at the time, and RL training runs were not covered. Findings describe the system as of July; OpenAI is reported to have addressed them. Takeaway for agent users: an agent's own approval layer is not a security boundary.
