Copeland XWEB EVO fridge controllers: 14 root-level flaws, exploitation attempts within two hours, five will never be fixed

VulnCheck disclosed 14 vulnerabilities in Copeland (Dixell) XWEB EVO refrigeration supervisors, each shown to give root. Nine hit pre-2021 firmware; five also hit the latest firmware, including a daily admin password derived from the device MAC address (which is exposed to unauthenticated users) and the date, enough to enable SSH and log in as root. Copeland ended support in March 2026 and will not fix them. VulnCheck saw exploitation attempts against CVE-2026-124014 under two hours after the CVEs went public on 12 November, then probes for CVE-2026-124010 on 13 November; it says these look opportunistic and are not linked to the October supermarket defrost attacks France blamed on Iran. About 2,000 XWEB EVO hosts are internet-exposed per Onyphe. Takeaway: pull any XWEB EVO off the internet now, put it behind a VPN, and plan a move to XWEB Pro.

Incidents

Coles, Australia's second-largest retailer, investigating a cyber security incident

Coles says it is working with the Australian Cyber Security Centre and outside experts to contain and secure its IT environment, and has so far found no evidence that customer, staff or supplier data was taken. Stores, online shopping and Flybuys (over nine million active members) are running; it notes Liquorland click-and-collect issues for a small number of customers without linking them to the incident. No group has claimed it and Coles has not said when it was discovered or whether there is a ransom demand. Takeaway: expect Coles- and Flybuys-themed phishing; early-stage, details may change.

Vectors

SANSAR: cryptominer for GPU clusters that rewrites NVIDIA telemetry to show 0% load

Elastic Security Labs tracks REF3364, which got in through an exposed Ray job agent, then installed a Rust orchestrator (SANSAR) controlled over Tor, an ftrace kernel rootkit that survives reboot via initramfs, and a modified NVML wrapper so nvidia-smi and other monitors report 0% GPU use with fake temperature and power readings. A separate backdoor, netns, pulls its C2 address from an Ethereum smart contract. Observed late October; Turkish comments in the rootkit, no attribution. Takeaway: if you run Ray or other GPU job schedulers, lock down who can submit jobs and verify NVML library integrity rather than trusting GPU dashboards.
