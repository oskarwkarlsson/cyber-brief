Exploited Outlook zero-day CVE-2026-124194 leads a record 1,235-flaw Patch Tuesday

Microsoft marks CVE-2026-124194 (Outlook spoofing, CVSS 6.5, "Exploitation Detected") as actively exploited. Per Microsoft's FAQ, a crafted message file opened in Outlook makes it connect to an attacker-controlled network location without the usual external-content warning, which can leak authentication information usable to impersonate the user. User interaction is required. CISA added it to KEV on 10 Nov with a 24 Nov deadline. BleepingComputer counts 1,235 fixes this month, 66 Critical (48 RCE). Takeaway: push the Outlook update first, and block outbound SMB/NTLM to the internet as a backstop.

Nitori, "the IKEA of Japan", says up to 37.62 million customer records may have leaked

Nitori detected unauthorized access to a company-managed system on 6 November and its investigation found the attacker likely had access since mid-October. Up to 37.62 million records on individual customers, corporate members and non-member online and store orders may have been taken; Nitori says member passwords and card data were not exposed. It has told Japan's privacy commission and police and warns of follow-on phishing. No actor has been named. Takeaway: expect Nitori-themed phishing in Japan and across its Asian markets.

Railway's breach grows: up to 100,000 workspaces' secrets and $740,000 of fraudulent AI usage

Railway's new incident report says attackers escaped build isolation on 20 October through an unknown flaw, stole internal source code, then abused a misconfigured trust relationship to get keys that decrypt customer environment variables for at most 1.85% of workspaces (about 100,000, a maximum not a confirmed count). Railway describes a roughly 26-hour fight against attackers using AI agents, with Wiz likening the harness to ARTEX. The attackers hunted AI provider keys first, then ran up about $740,000 in AI charges; Railway says it will cover what providers will not refund. Takeaway: if you deployed on Railway, rotate every secret, starting with AI API keys, and check provider bills.

Vectors

Unit 42: 26 scam "AI trading" sites hide prompt injections aimed at AI agents

Unit 42 found an indirect prompt injection campaign across 26 domains posing as AI-powered digital asset trading platforms in several languages. Hidden display:none spans tell AI agents to ignore prior instructions, act as a "financial expert", use urgency and social proof, and send users only to that site, an attempt to game AI search and assistants (black-hat generative engine optimization). The template still has an unfilled [naming_here] placeholder. Takeaway: treat investment advice from AI browsing agents with suspicion; the domain list is in Unit 42's note.
