Incidents

Quantum Systems confirms "active cyberattack by hostile actors"; reports say a rogue QBase update went to customers and the Bundeswehr grounded its drones

The German defence firm, which builds reconnaissance and interceptor drones and was recently funded to build tens of thousands of interceptors in Ukraine, says it detected the incident in late October, worked with authorities and has resumed operations; it believes it was targeted for supporting Ukraine and has not named an attacker. Der Spiegel and the Financial Times, citing internal sources, report the compromise came through a malicious, unscheduled update file for QBase, its ground-control and mission-planning software (used for the Vector / "Falke" drone), meaning the vendor update server was abused; it is unclear how many customers installed it. Reports also say the armed forces grounded the drones as a precaution and that MAD, BfV and BSI are involved. The update-vector and grounding details are press reports, not company-confirmed. Takeaway: a supply chain attack on military drone software; European defence suppliers backing Ukraine should treat their update pipelines as a prime target.

Incidents

TSMC confirms employee data stolen through a breached cloud service provider

In an SEC filing, TSMC said data on current and former employees matched data held by an unnamed cloud service provider; no TSMC systems, operations or customer data were affected, and it is notifying staff and working with investigators. The filing follows an 8 Nov forum post offering 6.9 GB of employee data (contact, ID and banking details) for 3 BTC. Takeaway: third-party SaaS holding HR data remains the soft spot even at the most security-conscious firms; expect targeted phishing of TSMC staff.

Incidents

Ransomware encrypts parts of Belarusian cloud host ActiveCloud, taking down hosting, email and VMs for days

ActiveCloud, which serves businesses in Belarus and Russia, disclosed the attack on Tuesday; its E-Cloud hosting, E-Cloud Protected and SmartMail email were hit, and outlets such as Smartpress and Domino's Belarus saw outages. It says main services are back and it found no evidence of data theft, but some customers still cannot restore VMs. No group has claimed it and the entry point is unknown. It follows the destructive Beget attack and the ESTT ransomware attack on Russian hosts this month, with no public link between them. Takeaway: hosting providers in the region are being hit one after another; if you rely on a single regional host, check your offsite backups.

Vectors

Mior and Skyfall: new attacks let a malicious cloud host read secrets inside AMD SEV-SNP confidential VMs

ETH Zurich's Mior corrupts the Reverse Map Table as SEV-SNP starts up by redirecting the security processor's writes to a PCIe device, then reads and writes guest memory (1,000 of 1,000 attempts) and can forge attestation reports; it needs hypervisor and BIOS control and was shown on a Zen 5 EPYC 9135. Skyfall, from ETH Zurich and Google, builds on AMD's Transient Scheduler Attacks to leak VM data. AMD lists firmware and microcode dated 12 Aug, with platform firmware to server makers in November (Skyfall) and December (Mior). Takeaway: lab attacks needing host control, so no direct risk to ordinary users, but confidential-computing assurances on AMD hosts depend on BIOS updates from your cloud or server vendor.
