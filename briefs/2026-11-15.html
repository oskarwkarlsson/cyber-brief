AI/Cloud

ARTEX goes mass-market: FBI flash alert, Amazon catches AI-key theft campaigns, and Cloudflare now tracks 50+ autonomous attack frameworks

The FBI says the open-source agentic pentest framework automates intrusions "at machine speed", some reaching privileged access within three hours, and keeps spreading through forks despite the original repo's removal on 8 Oct. Amazon's MadPot decoys caught ARTEX agents hunting exposed LLM gateways and AI builders (LiteLLM, Dify, Langflow, Open WebUI, Ollama and others), stealing provider keys to fund more hunting, plus deepfake KYC bypass at crypto exchanges (moderate-confidence Chinese-nexus attribution). Cloudflare's Cloudforce One says over 50 framework families scanned its customers in three days, ARTEX traffic is up 30x since 1 Oct, and in its own incident work attackers used static CI/CD tokens to pivot from hyperscalers into Cloudflare accounts, spinning up artex-* Workers and making 400 database dump requests in an hour. Takeaway: kill static non-expiring CI tokens, put admin panels and AI gateways behind identity, and rotate stored LLM keys.

Fireworks AI breach fallout widens: Cursor traces its Claude Code extension attack to a Fireworks-exposed key, and Uber notifies drivers of stolen licenses

Cursor says an attacker used a key it issued to Fireworks AI to copy old coding-agent traces from non-Privacy-Mode sessions; credentials left in those traces opened Cursor SaaS tools, archived non-production repos, support data and credentials users had sent to support, and enabled the malicious Claude Code 2.1.312/2.1.313 builds on 29 Oct (up to 5,000 users). Production, customer repos and signing keys were not affected, and Cursor has moved inference off Fireworks. Separately, Uber told regulators that Fireworks, which held some Uber data, was breached; stolen data includes driver's licenses of 2,567 people in Massachusetts, 3,430 in South Carolina and 1,873 in Rhode Island, with other states pending (TechCrunch named Fireworks, Uber confirmed). Fireworks blames a financially motivated group selling LLM access tokens; Notion was also hit. Takeaway: audit what data your AI inference vendors hold, and never paste secrets into agent sessions or support tickets.

Incidents

German drone maker Quantum Systems hit by "hostile actors"; reports say a rogue update to its QBase software reached customers and the Bundeswehr grounded drones

The defence firm, which supplies reconnaissance and interceptor drones and supports Ukraine, says it detected the attack in late October, worked with authorities and has resumed operations, and believes it was targeted for its Ukraine work; no attacker named. Der Spiegel and the Financial Times report the compromise came through a malicious unscheduled update for QBase, its mission-planning software, and that the armed forces grounded drones as a precaution. The update vector and grounding are press reports, not company-confirmed. Takeaway: European defence suppliers backing Ukraine should treat their update pipelines as a prime target.

CISA adds two exploited bugs with a Monday 16 Nov deadline: UnrealIRCd server-command injection and Check Point's Gaia root escalation

UnrealIRCd CVE-2026-114713 lets any ordinary user on a linked network inject server-to-server commands (KILL, GLINE and more); the maintainer calls it the worst issue in 15 years, found in an AI-assisted audit, and exploited since 8 Nov. Fix with hot-patch bundle-20261107 or 6.2.7.1, or a SPAMFILTER workaround on 6.0.4+. Check Point CVE-2026-123039 (DynamicCLI root socket) was used in the PHP2Shell gateway intrusions that ended with an ESXi wiper against Israeli organisations; hotfixes are out. Takeaway: patch both today if you run them, and hunt for rogue opers or unexpected root activity.
