AI/Cloud

OpenRouter off-boards three providers after prompt theft

Following Dragos's canary-token study (two million seeded prompts, July to November, with canary credentials later used by someone), OpenRouter says it off-boarded three inference providers, removed 125 endpoints from Zero Data Retention eligibility (some as a precaution), notified potentially affected customers and will run similar checks continuously. It says OpenRouter itself was not breached. Together AI, Novita and Phala have posted their own incident notices. Takeaway: ZDR labels on third-party inference are a claim, not a control; never put live secrets in prompts and review which providers your router can send data to.

FBI probes apparent breach of its NCIC crime database

CNN and NBC News, citing unnamed sources, and now an AP source via SecurityWeek, report the FBI is investigating an apparent hack of the National Crime Information Center, the database US police use for warrants, stolen property and background checks. The FBI has not confirmed it; no actor, scope or data loss has been disclosed. Takeaway: unconfirmed but potentially serious; watch for an official FBI or CJIS statement.

Cairn's AI-found zero-day: MONAI Label CVE-2026-133968, no fix yet

Amazon says China-linked operators let the Cairn autonomous framework find and exploit a path-traversal zero-day in an open-source medical imaging AI app during attacks on East Asian universities. The bug is now MONAI Label CVE-2026-133968 (CVSS 9.8), exploited in the wild, with no fixed release in the GitHub advisory as of Saturday. Takeaway: take internet-facing MONAI Label servers offline or put them behind auth until a release ships.

Exploited Entra Connect Health API flaw exposed tenant metadata

In notices to admins, Microsoft says a bug introduced in the Connect Health API on July 23 was exploited for read-only access to admin and technical-contact emails, tenant IDs, names, domains, license tiers and sync status until a server-side fix on Nov 19. No passwords, tokens or valid keys exposed, per Microsoft; the attacker and tenant count are not disclosed and there is no public advisory. Takeaway: expect targeted phishing at exposed admin addresses; enforce phishing-resistant MFA on privileged accounts.
