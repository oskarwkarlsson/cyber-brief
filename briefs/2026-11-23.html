Vulns

GL.iNet GoodCloud flaw exploited to backdoor routers and KVMs

GL.iNet says an unauthorized party exploited a vulnerability in its GoodCloud remote management service to install malware on some routers and KVM devices. The cloud side is fixed, but infected devices stay infected. BleepingComputer's look at the installer found persistence via startup scripts and scheduled tasks, planted SSH keys and a fake netstat hiding C2 traffic; users report root reverse shells. No CVE or root cause published, and no victim count. Takeaway: check for a process named crond -i /tmp/.log, consider switching off GoodCloud, update firmware and factory reset infected units without restoring old configs; treat machines behind an infected KVM as possibly compromised.

Kepco: second breach, about 1.52 million users

Korea Electric Power Corp. said on Sunday an external attacker stole data from two systems: 650,487 users of its Kepco On customer platform (names, resident registration numbers, bank account and card details, billing records) and 869,761 users of its SRM procurement system (corporate representatives and employees). Kepco found it on Friday, blocked external IPs, suspended services and is investigating with the National Intelligence Service. It comes a month after a separate leak of 35,528 people. Takeaway: part of a run of Korean breaches; expect phishing and fraud against Kepco customers.

Cleverbridge: payment card data copied, CVVs in some notices

Cleverbridge, the merchant-of-record platform behind software stores for brands like Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam and Jamf, says it detected unauthorized activity on Nov 4 and later found a third party copied stored payment card data. Notices from Nov 20 seen by BleepingComputer list card number, expiry date and CVV; other notices differ on CVVs. Not ransomware, per the company; entry point, dwell time and scale are undisclosed. Takeaway: if you bought software through a Cleverbridge checkout, watch card statements and consider asking your bank for a new card.

Samsung Securities confirms client data leak; Hyflock claims Samsung Group

Samsung Securities said Monday that unauthorized access to its customer response website, detected Saturday, leaked names, addresses and other personal data of 8,241 clients, and it has reported it to authorities (Yonhap). Separately, the Hyflock group listed Samsung Group on its leak site on Nov 22, claiming ESX admin access, domain controller compromise, 1 TB stolen and systems encrypted (claim, unverified; Samsung has not commented and no link to the Securities incident is known). Takeaway: two distinct stories; only the brokerage breach is confirmed.
