Crédit Mutuel Arkéa confirms a heist of roughly $133 million through a supplier

The French banking group says a "sophisticated attack" went through a service provider's platform and let attackers make transfers from the bank's own account to other banks, from where the money was potentially converted to crypto. Arkéa would not confirm ZachXBT's estimate of more than $133 million but said it could "validate the order of magnitude". It says customer accounts were not affected and disputes reports that entry was via its Fortuneo subsidiary; the supplier is unnamed. Takeaway: banks should review what third-party platforms can initiate payments from their own treasury accounts.

Zyxel GS1900 switch zero-day was behind the Kapibala attacks on 996 devices

Zyxel patched CVE-2026-107441, an unauthenticated out-of-bounds write in the web interface of ten GS1900 switch models that gives OS command execution. GreyNoise had blamed the Kapibala campaign (996 switches in 48 countries since about August 17, stealing configs and root hashes) on a June-patched bug, but Binarly found the exploit worked against firmware patched for that one. Fixed firmware shipped October 22, the advisory November 3. It is not in CISA KEV yet. Takeaway: GS1900 owners who patched in June are still exposed; update, keep management off the internet and rotate credentials.

APT29 watering holes pushed zero-click n-day chains to iPhones and Android phones

Sekoia attributes, with moderate-to-high confidence, a watering hole operation to APT29 (Midnight Blizzard) using compromised sites of a Ukrainian gas producer, a US chemical trade association and a Bulgarian military-related site, plus a tampered copy of Ukrainian analytics service Plerdy's tracker that thousands of sites load. Visitors were profiled and served matching chains for iOS 16.7 and 18.5 and Chrome for Android 139 to 145, stealing cookies, WhatsApp and Telegram data, Keychain and photos. Takeaway: every flaw used is patched, so outdated phones are the exposure; push iOS and Chrome updates on managed devices.

AI/Cloud

OpenAI pipeline produced 948 working Linux root exploits in one $1.2 million run

OpenAI says an agentic pipeline built on an upcoming cyber model analysed 2,308 candidate Linux kernel findings in a mid-October run, reproduced 1,724 vulnerabilities within 30 hours and produced 948 working local privilege escalation exploits against a stock desktop distribution, at $1,279 per exploit in API credits. It generates candidate patches and humans review everything before reporting to maintainers; OpenAI says agents found at least 10 novel kernel exploitation techniques. Takeaway: in OpenAI's words, a low-privileged account on an unpatched OS should be treated as functionally equivalent to root against a well-resourced attacker.
