OpenSSH 10.6p2: critical password authentication bypass on musl-based Linux such as Alpine

Portable OpenSSH 10.6p2, released 5 Nov, works around a password authentication bypass that hits Linux systems built and run without PAM, notably Alpine, OpenWrt, Buildroot and Yocto. Researcher Özgün Kültekin (Wakefield Labs), who found it with AI agents, shows that a password of 257 to 1,024 bytes can log an attacker into accounts whose shadow hash is "*", including key-only accounts, as long as password logins are on and at least one other account has a real password. Alpine's stock openssh-server has PAM off; its AWS and Azure images ship an "alpine" account with passwordless doas. Glibc distros and PAM setups are not affected. No exploitation reported yet. Takeaway: patch, or set PasswordAuthentication no on any musl-based box now.

DigiCert breach: archived customer data and some identity documents accessed

The certificate authority says a February 2025 server migration left an internal system, previously VPN-only, open to the internet with self-registration enabled, and an authorization flaw let outside accounts read archived customer data. An unauthorized account accessed names, business emails, phones and addresses between 27 June and 2 July 2026, and for some people driver's licences, passports or utility bills submitted for validation. DigiCert first spotted the accounts in June but only confirmed data access on 19 October. It says certificate issuance, CertCentral and DigiCert ONE were not compromised, and has not said how many people are affected. Takeaway: if you validated certificates with DigiCert, expect targeted phishing using your real details.

Queens Public Library: ransomware knocks out Wi-Fi and digital services across 66 branches

The Queens Public Library in New York City, 66 branches serving about 2.3 million residents, confirmed a ransomware attack that encrypted systems and took down on-site computers, checkout and return machines, Wi-Fi, digital media services and the mobile app. Branches remain open; some programs may be hit. The system first flagged outages Wednesday afternoon and confirmed ransomware later that day. It says it is working with law enforcement and cybersecurity experts. No group had claimed responsibility as of Thursday evening, and no data theft has been confirmed. Takeaway: treat QPL-related phishing as high risk while recovery runs, and assume library-system playbooks remain a live ransomware target.

Fake OpenAI, SpaceX and Anthropic event pages chain Kali365 device-code phishing with VoidProxy AiTM

Push Security (5 Nov) found fake registration pages for an OpenAI Academy livestream, a private SpaceX AI briefing and Anthropic at AWS re:Invent that demand a work-account sign-in for AI rewards. Microsoft path: Kali365 device-code phishing, then a VoidProxy Microsoft AiTM page. Google path: VoidProxy reverse-proxy AiTM that steals credentials, MFA and session cookies. Push tracks the lure kits as ZoomMock and MSDRoot (MSDRoot from about 22 Oct); delivery uses Google and Zapier short links plus Cloudflare bot checks. Earlier related reporting came from ANY.RUN (Jul) and Cofense (29 Oct). Takeaway: treat AI-branded event invites that require Microsoft or Google sign-in as phishing until proven otherwise, and watch device-code flows.
