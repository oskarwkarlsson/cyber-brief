Attackers hijacked cPGuard's update channel and stole root secrets from nearly 3,000 Linux web servers, CISA confirms the WordPress core upgrade-window RCE is exploited, and WorkOS says an ARTEX AI-driven attacker got into its sales app

Vectors

cPGuard update channel pushed a backdoor and data stealer

OpsShield says an attacker used compromised credentials to get into its update CDN on November 2 at 02:06 UTC and shipped malicious cPGuard versions 5.88.12 to 5.88.19 for under 12 hours. The script grabbed Linux password hashes, root MariaDB/MySQL config, root shell history and root SSH authorized_keys and known_hosts, and uploaded them through OpsShield's own licensing server. A spokesperson told BleepingComputer 2,946 servers were affected and the billing database was copied too. cPGuard is a hosting security suite used with cPanel, DirectAdmin and Plesk, and Namecheap offers it. Takeaway: update to 5.88.25 or later, then rotate passwords, database credentials, cloud secrets and SSH keys; the update removes the backdoor but not the theft.

CISA adds WordPress core CVE-2026-119977 to KEV, due Friday

The upgrade-window race condition (WordPress 4.7 to 7.1.3) lets unauthenticated attackers run code during a full-package core upgrade because staging paths are predictable and web-reachable. Last week it was mass-scanning with no confirmed compromise; CISA now lists it as exploited, flags it for forensic triage and gives federal agencies until November 6. Takeaway: make sure every site is on 7.1.4 and check sites that upgraded recently for unexpected files.

WorkOS breach: ARTEX AI used against an internal sales app

The SSO and identity API provider (customers include OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor, Perplexity and Snowflake) found unauthorized access on October 30 to a sales tool holding business contact records and summaries of customer communications. It says no app data or production services were touched. Founder Michael Grinich says the attacker used the ARTEX AI pentest suite, seen in user agents "artex-enrich/1.0" and "norma/0.4". Takeaway: expect targeted phishing aimed at WorkOS customers' admins; this adds to October's run of AI developer platform breaches.

AI/Cloud

Google: LLMjacking now costs some victims $100,000 a day

GTIG and Mandiant describe two clusters, one scanning for exposed LLM endpoints and one stealing API keys from machines, which sell access to "LLM mixer" resale services. One actor ran over 500,000 calls in under eight hours to feed the jet-night.com token router, passing nearly $1 million in charges to a single victim. Google also names a cluster UNC6993. Takeaway: put spend alerts and quotas on every AI provider key and keep model endpoints off the open internet.
