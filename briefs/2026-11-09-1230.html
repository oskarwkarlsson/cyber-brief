LNbits: stop all servers now, patches due within 24 to 48 hours

The open-source Bitcoin Lightning wallet project LNbits told all users on Sunday (13:56 UTC) to stop their LNbits servers immediately "following a wave of attacks", saying it has several security patches to push. About 80 minutes earlier it had asked operators only to disable the Paywall extension while it investigated a possible bug. Lead developer Ben Arc said patches should be merged within 24 to 48 hours, and LNbits later disabled every instance on its own hosted service, promising to resume within 24 hours. The Hacker News cites one operator who says new accounts used an exploit to withdraw 2,285,545 sats (about 0.023 BTC) before he stopped it; that loss figure is single-source. Takeaway: if you or anyone you know runs LNbits, keep it offline until the fixed release is out and check balances and new accounts.

BOSSAI: a single operator's 12-agent AI mesh hit emergency services in Taiwan

Acronis's Threat Research Unit (4 November, picked up today) describes one operator, attributed with what it calls high confidence to Hualien, Taiwan, who wired 12 AI agents into an attack mesh and pointed it at hundreds of organizations in 11 countries: emergency services, banks, universities, military systems and more. Commercial models (it names Grok, OpenClaw, Claude Code and Codex) were used to build the infrastructure but refused offensive tasks, so the attacking agents ran almost entirely on "abliterated" DeepSeek, GLM and Qwen variants with safety removed. Confirmed harm: case records across seven Taipei districts pulled from a 119 emergency dispatch API, and a fire-department patient-care platform exposing 2018 ECG readings, ambulance video and vitals with no login. Takeaway: AI agents let a lone actor scan and exploit at the scale of a team; unauthenticated APIs and forgotten systems are what they find first.

Amplifon Americas: 1.3 million people's health and ID data stolen

The US arm of hearing-care group Amplifon says hackers got into internal systems in January 2026 and copied files between 18 January and 8 February. After an eight-month review it found names, contact details, driver's license and other ID numbers, dates of birth, hearing and medical information and health insurance details, plus Social Security numbers and financial data for some people. SecurityWeek puts the total above 1.3 million based on the Texas filing. Amplifon says it knows of no misuse. No group has been named.

Frontline Education breach more than doubles to 3.09 million

Frontline Education, the K-12 school administration software maker, told the Indiana attorney general that its August breach affected 3,085,266 people, well over the roughly 1.3 million in earlier state filings that we covered on 3 and 4 November. Attackers exploited a flaw in an unnamed third-party product and took staff and student data from Frontline Central and School Health Management, including SSNs, dates of birth, government ID numbers, Medicaid IDs and student IDs.
