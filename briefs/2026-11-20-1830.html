AI/Cloud

Canary prompts reveal three compromised AI providers

Dragos (HD Moore's research team) sent about two million prompts with unique URLs and canary AWS credentials to widely used AI services from July to November 2026. Seeded secrets were later used against its test systems and canary AWS accounts, which Dragos reads as evidence that data from at least three providers was exposed. It also found inference-marketplace resellers retaining customer prompts through standard logging, and providers apparently ignoring privacy settings, including prompts to US providers reaching Chinese AI organizations. Dragos does not name the providers in the summary we read. Takeaway: never put live secrets in prompts; treat third-party inference, especially via resellers, as a data-exposure path.

Bazuka: modular Android fraud framework with LLM control

ThreatFabric found Bazuka in late October while investigating an NFC-relay banking trojan. It is a full framework: a dropper, a core and 23 modules for overlays, keylogging, 2FA and SMS interception, remote control and crypto-address swapping, plus LLM integration for managing victims and ADB bridging. A new trick screenshots the home or lock screen and shows it as an overlay to hide what runs underneath. Takeaway: mobile banking fraud is getting more automated; sideloaded apps remain the entry point.

WhatsApp fixes Android flaw a scammer abused

WhatsApp patched CVE-2026-23871 in WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business for Android before 2.26.17.4: incomplete validation of Click to WhatsApp ad-reply URLs could reveal a target's IP address when a crafted message rendered. WhatsApp found it while disrupting a scam campaign and believes a scammer used it to make messages seem more credible. It also fixed two medium flaws (CVE-2026-102769, CVE-2026-44908) with no sign of exploitation. Takeaway: low impact but exploited; keep WhatsApp updated.
