Active campaign abuses cloud-hosted ScreenConnect to hit MSPs and their customers

Huntress says a campaign that began around 18:47 CET Wednesday has compromised 6 MSPs and 286+ endpoints across 47 organisations, and the numbers are climbing. Attackers use ScreenConnect's Run Command to install their own ScreenConnect clients on managed devices, giving access outside the MSP's instance. Huntress also warns of a "reverse pivot" where a technician's workstation can be compromised by opening a session to an infected device; whether that is a software bug or feature abuse is unknown. Telemetry points to stolen credentials and hijacked support sessions, and sequential IDs suggest earlier victims. ConnectWise confirms it is investigating "a limited number" of partners. Takeaway: if you or your MSP use ScreenConnect, review Run Command history, revoke sessions, reset credentials from a clean device, verify MFA, and do not open interactive sessions to suspect machines.

Fireworks AI breach reaches Notion: AI chat transcripts exposed for under 1% of workspaces

Notion's advisory says the dataset Fireworks confirmed on 8 November included Notion AI chat transcripts used for its Insights feature. Most were "unattributable" (no user or workspace labels, no tool-call results), but the text itself may hold whatever people pasted in. Notion has disabled Insights, stopped serving open-weight models via Fireworks, and contacted affected customers. Fireworks says the second intrusion came via a third-party cloud provider hit on 23 October. This materially widens a story we covered on 10 November: the breach now touches end users of a downstream SaaS. Takeaway: if Notion contacted you, rotate any passwords or API keys ever pasted into Notion AI, and ask your AI vendors which subprocessors store prompts.

Krebs ties the admin of Qilin, the most active ransomware program, to a man in St. Petersburg

KrebsOnSecurity follows old handles of Qilin's leader "Haise" through RAMP forum messages indexed by Intel 471 (RAMP was seized by the FBI in January 2026) and points to a 36-year-old man in St. Petersburg. Citing NCC Group, Krebs says Qilin claimed at least 365 victims in three months, pays affiliates at least 80%, and works with English-speaking affiliates. No arrest or charges reported. Takeaway: attribution, not a new technique, but Qilin remains the top ransomware threat by volume.
