Mercado Bitcoin suspends deposits and withdrawals after a cyberattack

Latin America's largest digital asset platform, with about 4.5 million customers, confirmed a cybersecurity incident on Monday morning and froze deposits and withdrawals. Blockchain security firms say more than $46 million left the platform late on Sunday night; the company has not confirmed that figure. Mercado Bitcoin says customers were not harmed and no data was breached, but several researchers point to on-chain evidence that some customer wallets were drained. Tether froze about $4.5 million and Chainalysis says it led freezes of nearly $10 million in total. No attribution yet. It comes days after the Binance theft the FBI pinned on North Korea.

Vapor Panda's ARTEX AI campaign grows to 200 organizations in 14 countries

CrowdStrike now says the China-nexus actor used the ARTEX multi-agent pentest framework, steered by DeepSeek and a second commercial model, against more than 200 organizations since Oct 17, up from the roughly 140 targets reported last week. At least nine were compromised, including US and Belgian telecoms, government bodies in Greece, the Middle East and Latin America, and US pharma firms. In Greece the agents forged an admin token from old mail-server keys and read mailboxes; operators told agents to skip Falcon-protected hosts. Separately, DIVD says the September attack on its own network was ARTEX acting autonomously, from initial access to root in 12 minutes via two zero-days.

BYOD ransomware gang claims Michigan State University

The gang behind last week's University of New Mexico attack, which forced its hospital to divert ambulances, added Michigan State to its leak site on Sunday and says it stole personal data on 1.3 million current and former students (claim, unverified). MSU says the claim ties to suspicious activity in early October that led it to force password resets for every NetID, and that current evidence shows no compromise of its central Active Directory and no other data accessed.
