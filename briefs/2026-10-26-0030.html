Tailworm report shows a single hacked device on a Tailscale network can reach root on other servers via Tailscale SSH forwarding, with no CVE or fix yet

New risks since 18:30
Vulns

Unpatched Tailscale SSH flaws turn one compromised node into root on reachable servers

Erica Windisch of Cyberdione Labs published "Tailworm" on Oct 25, a lab assessment of Tailscale 1.104.2 and headscale 0.29.5. Where Tailscale SSH allows port forwarding, an attacker who already controls one device that the ACLs let connect to a target can abuse unix-socket forwarding (ssh -L and -R) so the root tailscaled process dials or replaces root-only sockets, plus a race that defeats the path allowlist. The report rates five findings High, including a conn25 app-connector egress flaw and a localapi exposure via serve. No passwords or keys are needed, only an initial foothold. There is no CVE, bulletin or fixed version yet; Tailscale's VP of engineering said on Reddit the company is fixing issues it found and is not recommending everyone disable Tailscale SSH. Takeaway: on the hosted service, turn off forwarding in SSH rules and tighten who can SSH where; on headscale 0.29.5 forwarding cannot be disabled, so treat every SSH grant as root on the target.
