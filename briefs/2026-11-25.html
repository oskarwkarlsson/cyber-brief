Actors

GT13 owns Check Point management servers at 22 orgs

GreyNoise tracked an actor it calls GT13 inside 24 Check Point Security Management servers at 22 organizations in 17 countries (government, finance, energy, transport, mining, manufacturing) from Oct 17 to Nov 2. Initial access is unknown; after getting in it used SmartConsole auth bypass CVE-2026-16232 and tried Citrix, Ivanti, NetLogon and Linux bugs, relaying through a hijacked Rejetto HFS server. GreyNoise believes agentic AI tooling drove it, but AI use is unconfirmed. Likely still active; IoCs published. Takeaway: Check Point admins should hunt with those IoCs now.

Vulns

CISA KEV: Drupal, Optimizely, Idunno AntiCsrf

Three exploited flaws added Nov 24, federal deadline Nov 27. Drupal core CVE-2026-132165 (anonymous install.php abuse for site takeover or code execution), patched Nov 19 with no attacks then, is now confirmed exploited. Optimizely Web Experimentation CVE-2026-132224 is the signup-flow flaw behind the Project JavaScript skimmer injections. Idunno AntiCsrf CVE-2026-131914 is the dead ASP.NET library with no fix; CISA says stop using it. Takeaway: upgrade Drupal to 10.6.22, 11.3.22 or 11.4.13, or block /core/install.php.

Incidents

Radware breach exposes customer cloud keys

Per a root cause report seen by BleepingComputer (shared by an anonymous source), an internal Radware Cloud WAF management API sat on the internet with no authentication and was accessed on Nov 19, ten hours before detection. Exposed: customer contact details and, for some, credentials for log export to AWS S3, Azure Blob Storage or SFTP. Takeaway: Radware Cloud WAF or Bot customers should rotate export keys and SAS tokens and check storage logs since Nov 19.

Iran suspected in up to 50 tanker hacks

The Wall Street Journal reports US officials suspect Iranian hackers in as many as 50 cyberattacks on vessels carrying oil, liquefied gas and other hazardous cargo across Asia and Europe in recent weeks; some US-bound tankers in August had navigation systems breached, and FBI and Coast Guard teams boarded them. Officials are looking at the "FortiBleed" Fortinet router bug, which a September FBI and Coast Guard alert flagged on ships. Reported via TechCrunch; the FBI did not comment and the count is unconfirmed.
