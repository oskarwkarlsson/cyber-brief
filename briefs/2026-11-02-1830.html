Supermarket freezers hacked again across Europe, including Sweden

In the early hours of Monday, attackers remotely changed temperature settings on supermarket refrigeration systems, with retailers saying freezers were set to 50°C and staff unable to change them back. Retailers report a limited number of smaller stores in Sweden, more than 50 in Belgium and over a dozen in Ireland, Estonia, the Netherlands and Luxembourg. It follows the October attack on about 700 French victims through internet-exposed refrigeration remote controllers. No one has claimed either wave, and it is not known whether they are linked. CERT-SE says it is in ongoing contact with affected Swedish businesses. If you run connected cooling or building controllers, take their remote interfaces off the internet.

Crypto

BTCPay Server hot wallets drained on outdated instances

BTCPay Server says it got reports on Nov 1 of Bitcoin and Litecoin being stolen from hot wallets on self-hosted servers running version 2.4.1 or earlier. The attackers used a different method to exploit the bug fixed in 2.4.2 back in August. Wallets whose spending keys are held off the server, such as hardware wallets, were not affected. BTCPay says updating alone cannot remove access an attacker may already have, so older servers should be rebuilt on a fresh machine.

AI/Cloud

Langflow: two 9.8 flaws give an unauthenticated attacker a superuser session and code execution

IBM's bulletin covers CVE-2026-93680 (authentication bypass) and CVE-2026-110186 (remote code execution, reachable through the Model Context Protocol tool listing) in the open-source AI agent builder. Every release from 1.0.0 through 1.12.8 is affected and 1.13.0 is the only fix; IBM gives no workaround. A public Metasploit module already uses the auth bypass to list a server's tools, though it does not run code. Not on CISA KEV and no confirmed exploitation yet, but six earlier Langflow bugs ended up there.

Actors

Google dismantles a 1.3 million-device Android TV box botnet and blames the maker

Google says it has high confidence that Shenzhen OEM Videostrong built the XCPE backdoor into its set-top boxes on purpose and sold access to criminals, who used the boxes for ad fraud and residential proxies and to load more malware. Google took down the control servers and the control domains of affiliate malware families, with Japan's National Police Agency, Lumen, HUMAN, Plume, Synthient and TrendAI, and has banned Videostrong from certified Android. The backdoor stays on the devices and owners cannot remove it.
