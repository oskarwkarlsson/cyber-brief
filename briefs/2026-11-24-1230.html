Peninsula Energy: incident hits US uranium mine

The ASX-listed uranium miner requested a trading halt after a cybersecurity incident on Friday night at its Lance Project in Wyoming. Its filing says the incident breached its "operating system" and will temporarily affect operations, while administrative systems in the US and Australia were not affected. It has not said which systems were hit, whether production stopped, or who was behind it; no group has claimed it. The halt runs until Nov 25 trading or an announcement.

WHMCS: unauthenticated SQLi plus a 9.0 RCE

The billing platform used by many web hosts disclosed three flaws on Nov 23 with fixes in 9.0.10, 8.13.8 and 9.1.2. CVE-2026-82981 (CVSS 4.0 8.7) lets anyone reach the shopping cart and pull client records and admin password hashes with no login. CVE-2026-93700 (CVSS 3.1 9.0) lets a logged-in client read config files including database credentials and run code; the advisory also says to delete existing email campaigns and empty the template cache. CVE-2026-82979 (7.1) is an authenticated SQLi. No exploitation reported; self-hosted installs must update, 8.0 to 8.12 has no fix.

Two open databases expose 3.5 billion records

Researchers told WIRED of two unrelated unsecured databases. Jeremiah Fowler found about 2 billion credentials that look like infostealer output, with cookies, session tokens and about 400,000 detailed victim profiles; the host took it down. UpGuard's Greg Pollock found 1.64 billion aggregated records, mostly China-linked, including about 840 million distinct national ID numbers; it went offline during his investigation. Owners unknown and neither dataset fully deduplicated. Stolen session cookies can bypass MFA, so revoke sessions after infostealer hits.

UK NCSC: marked rise in agentic AI attacks

The NCSC says that since September it has seen incidents across multiple sectors where attackers used agentic offensive frameworks (it names ARTEX, plus Cairn, PentAGI, RedAmon, Xalgorix and Darkmoon) to find and exploit internet-facing weaknesses in quick succession, causing downtime or footholds. The weaknesses are old ones: committed credentials, IaC misconfigurations, default or missing authentication and API flaws. What is new is speed and low cost, often with cheap open models or illegal API resellers. The tools are noisy for now, which helps detection.
