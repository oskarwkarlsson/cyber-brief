Volt Typhoon ran four confirmed zero-day campaigns through a covert relay network Lumen calls Silent Train

Lumen's Black Lotus Labs says it has tracked and quietly disrupted Silent Train since late 2023: rapidly rotating chains of compromised SOHO, IoT and enterprise devices, exploited on demand, cleaned up after each operation and refreshed. It notified hundreds of victims in telecoms, government, defense, satellite, water, energy, transport and other critical sectors. Confirmed zero-day use includes Versa Director (CVE-2024-39717), a chained pair CVE-2025-8875/8876, Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN (CVE-2026-20127, exploited well after its February 2026 disclosure) and VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-16812), plus a likely Quest KACE zero-day. Takeaway: management, SD-WAN and out-of-band admin planes are the prime target; keep them patched and log them.

Vulns

CISA adds VMware ESX CVE-2026-41709 to KEV: admin actions can go unlogged

CISA added the Broadcom VMware ESX insufficient-logging flaw on Oct 28, saying a malicious actor with admin access could perform certain operations without being logged. Federal deadline is Nov 11; ransomware use is listed as unknown. It is not an entry point on its own, but it helps an intruder who already has host admin hide their tracks, so apply Broadcom's fix and review ESX admin access. CISA also added Fragnesia (CVE-2026-46300, Linux kernel), already covered in the 18:30 alert.

"Icepick" leak exposes details of more than 41,000 DHS employees and contractors, including undercover ICE staff

WIRED reports DDoSecrets published records on DHS and seven component agencies including ICE and CBP: emails, phone numbers, office addresses, facility locations, internal system references and members of groups tied to wiretap and surveillance programs, last updated Aug 19, 2026. DDoSecrets says the data came from DHS's Okta login system via a group calling itself the Department of Peace; Okta says it has no evidence its systems were compromised. The source of the data is not independently confirmed.

Japan Airlines says a breach at Amadeus exposed reservation data on 92,000 customers

JAL says its reservation, ticketing and check-in provider Amadeus told it last week that hackers accessed part of the customer reservation data for JAL and Japan Transocean Air: names, reservation codes and flight details, plus passport numbers, frequent flyer numbers, emails and phones for about 2,000 people. No card data or flight operations were affected. It adds to a run of Japanese breaches (Capcom, Burger King Japan, a Sony-owned ISP, GMO) that led PM Takaichi to promise emergency measures. Whether other Amadeus airline customers were hit is not yet known; expect airline-themed phishing.
