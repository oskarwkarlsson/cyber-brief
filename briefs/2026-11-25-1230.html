Hex.pm registry servers hacked via CVSS 10 flaw

On Nov 23 an attacker exploited CVE-2026-129514 (CVSS 10.0) in hex.pm, the Elixir and Erlang package registry, and ran OS commands on six production web servers. The bug sat in the pre-auth Erlang-format request parser since Feb 23, 2016; a fix was live within about 20 minutes. Hex found no package or database changes, but database reads are not logged, so emails, password hashes, API key hashes, 2FA secrets and sessions may have been read. All storage, CDN, email and database credentials and session signing keys were rotated, logging everyone out; the registry signing key is not yet rotated. Takeaway: if you have a hex.pm account, change your password and reset 2FA; fetch only from repo.hex.pm, not mirrors, until new clients ship.

FinCEN takes systems offline after intrusion

A banner on the Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing site says Treasury's FinCEN is investigating a cybersecurity incident that "resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems" and has taken some systems offline. BSA E-Filing stays up but some services may be unavailable; deadlines are extended for affected filers and banks are told to use a hotline for urgent suspicious activity reports. No attribution and no claim of responsibility; Treasury did not comment. First reported by The Verge.

CIA breach reportedly used Artex AI platform

Update to the CIA story: The New York Times and Nextgov report that the CIA and at least two other US spy agencies had unclassified information exposed, and that the hackers allegedly used Artex, a Chinese-made platform that wraps any large language model to automate multiple intrusion stages. Reported via The Record; anonymous sources, no official confirmation of the Artex link or the other agencies. Artex is the same toolkit Wiz and the UK NCSC tie to the recent surge of agentic intrusions.
