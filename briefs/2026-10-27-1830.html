AI/Cloud

Warp discloses a breach of a third-party vendor that exposed AI agent logs and telemetry

Warp CEO Zach Lloyd says the company spotted "active and narrow-banded exploitation" of a vendor in its infrastructure on Friday and estimates roughly 1% of its users may be affected. Agent decision traces and telemetry were exposed; Warp has not said whether prompts, code or credentials were included. Lloyd attributes the attack to "an agent swarm" using a sophisticated escalation technique. CrowdStrike is investigating. It follows last week's OpenCode API key breach. If you use Warp, rotate any secrets that may have appeared in agent sessions.

Israeli zero-click spyware vendor reportedly top bidder on Starlink-targeted ads in 105 countries

Citizen Lab's John Scott-Railton says data from Trend Micro research suggests a worldwide ad-based surveillance operation against Starlink users, run via targeted ads from Discover-Tech, a company Citizen Lab previously caught delivering zero-click infections. Trend's report labels the actor SHADOW-EARTH-79 without naming the company; the attribution link is Citizen Lab's. Practical advice from the report: block targeted ads.

ZloyRAT Android trojan spreads via fake fuel-finder, VPN and drone-radar apps in Russia

F6 researchers found three lure apps (a traffic police and fuel-stock map, a fake government "GosuVPN" and "Drone Radar") promoted from a site registered in early October. The trojan grabs SMS, notifications and contacts, can send messages and place calls, and targets 60+ Russian banking and finance apps. Actor unknown. A reminder that crisis-themed apps are a fast-moving lure.

Leaked SquareML access key exposed data of 238,083 Denver Health Medical Plan members

The no-code healthcare analytics vendor told DHMP that a read-only database key had sat on a public website from June 27, 2025 until it was revoked on August 18, 2026. Names, birth dates, demographics, health metrics, eligibility and admission and discharge data may have been accessed; no SSNs or financial data. DHMP says it has no evidence of misuse. Fourteen months of exposure is the headline lesson on secret scanning.
