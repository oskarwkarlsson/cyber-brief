Chainalysis says attackers using AI agents broke into its internal tools and saw customer names and emails, and Talos finds a stealer that asks an LLM to rank what each victim is worth

Top stories
Incidents

Chainalysis breached by an outside actor using "offensive agentic techniques"; customer user IDs, names, emails and role labels accessed

The blockchain analytics firm, whose tools are used by law enforcement, exchanges and banks, disclosed late Friday (US time) that it detected the intrusion earlier this month and has been patching and hardening since. It says it has found no evidence that investigation content, authentication credentials or session tokens were accessed, and that the incident is unrelated to the Binance theft. No technical detail yet on the flaws used, tools reached or dwell time. Customers are told to enable 2FA and watch for fake password resets and scammers posing as Chainalysis, especially "fund recovery" or recruiting pitches. Confirmed by a Chainalysis spokesperson to BleepingComputer; The Hacker News quotes the same customer email.

Vectors

VOLTA infostealer sends each victim's stolen haul to an LLM for a "what they're worth" target assessment

Talos describes a self-deleting Windows stealer (self-named VOLTA-AUTODOXER) that grabs browser passwords, cookies, cards, wallet seeds, Discord, Telegram, Slack and Microsoft OAuth tokens, SSH and cloud keys and Wi-Fi passwords, then uses the stolen Gmail session to search receipts for a home address. The bundle goes to OpenRouter with a fallback chain of three models if one refuses. It looks like a builder sold to customers. Defender tip from Talos: block or alert on Chrome launched with --headless from unexpected processes, which cuts its App-Bound Encryption bypass.

Vulns

Public PoC for Android "SMS of Death" (CVE-2026-58882): one 8-byte text crashes the phone process and can drop emergency calls

A researcher released an exploit for a zero-click denial of service in Android's WAP Push handling. Repeated messages can cause reboot loops, and one Android 12 test phone needed a factory reset. Google reportedly fixed it in October updates for Android 14 to 17, though it is not listed in the October bulletin, and Android 12 and 13 stay unpatched. Not reported as exploited in the wild. Update Android devices now.
