N0n leak site lists Expedia Group with a sweeping data-theft claim (claim, unverified)

The N0n extortion group added Expedia Group to its leak site at 00:52 UTC today (01:52 CET), per ransomware.live, marked "NOT PAID, EXPOSED". The listing claims loyalty tables of about 830 million rows across 35 programs, a 58.4 million-row traveler profile table, a 333 million-row account archive pairing traveler IDs and emails with "password like tokens", 222 GB of booking events, Vrbo reservation and payment records, and, most worrying if true, live credentials for Expedia's big-data platform including a source-code token for 19,935 private repositories and production cloud access across five more cloud accounts. None of this is verified: no sample has been authenticated, no media outlet has confirmed it and no Expedia statement was found this run. N0n is the busiest leak site this month (50 of 279 November claims, per HackerFeeds), and groups do inflate. Takeaway: if you rely on Expedia, Hotels.com or Vrbo logins, use a unique password and MFA now and treat travel-themed phishing with extra suspicion; partners integrating with Expedia APIs should watch for a statement and be ready to rotate shared keys.

Web3Auth flaw exposed Keplr social-login private keys; Web3Auth swept about $5.6 million to safety

A flaw at Web3Auth, the Consensys-owned service behind Keplr's Google and Apple sign-in, exposed private keys for some of those wallets; Keplr co-founder Josh Lee put it at about 20% of Google/Apple-login accounts and said it was not a bug in Keplr's code. Web3Auth disclosed on Friday, paused social-login wallet creation, then on Saturday began emptying still-vulnerable wallets "using the same path of the original attacker"; early Sunday it published 12 recovery addresses that held about $5.6 million on Sunday per The Block, mostly about 2.49 million ATOM (staked ATOM converted to liquid tokens to skip the 21-day unbonding). Bitquery had counted about $6.01 million moved from roughly 44,700 keys across Cosmos Hub, Osmosis, Injective and other chains and read it as theft, but all eight addresses it tied to the thief are on Web3Auth's recovery list. Web3Auth's mention of an "original attacker" implies some funds were stolen, but it has not said how much. Recovery-phrase and hardware-wallet users are not affected; Web3Auth says no evidence MetaMask users were hit. Takeaway: Keplr Google/Apple-login users should not deposit, should move any remaining funds to a seed-phrase or hardware wallet, and should expect a reduced balance that may be in custody, not lost.

Iris Plaza: third-party cloud breach may have exposed about 7.7 million people (recap of last night's alert)

Iris Plaza, which runs household-goods maker Iris Ohyama's official online store, says data held on an external cloud service may have been viewed or taken: about 5.8 million current and former members (names, addresses, phones, emails, gender, dates of birth and, for current members, encrypted passwords of an unstated scheme) and about 1.9 million non-members such as guest buyers. Card data was not on the affected service and marketplace shops were not hit. It found a suspicious file on 28 October, shut the store and related Iris Tech Service sites that day, and told police and Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on 29 October; as of 5 November there were no reports of misuse, and the number of records actually taken is still being worked out. A spokesperson told BleepingComputer there has been no direct contact from the attacker and the perpetrators are unidentified. First covered in yesterday's 18:30 alert; nothing new overnight. Takeaway: Iris Plaza users should change any reused password and treat unexpected "Iris Plaza" emails or texts as phishing until the company contacts them directly.

Veeam ships kernel fixes for Linux CVE-2026-98432 across its appliances; 12.3 helper appliances still waiting

Veeam's KB4966 (6 Nov, flagged by HKCERT today) says every Veeam component that carries its own Linux kernel is exposed to CVE-2026-98432, the CVSS 9.8 use-after-free in Linux TCP/IPv4 connection setup that this brief first covered on 29 Oct as a remote-root risk. Veeam Software Appliance and Infrastructure Appliance 13.1 (proxies, hardened repositories, Cloud Connect gateways and more) have had a patched kernel (5.14.0-570.60.1+27.2.el9_6_ciq) in the Veeam repository since 2 Nov; with default settings mandatory updates install automatically on the nearest Sunday at 14:00 in the backup server's time zone, so default-configured appliances should have taken it yesterday. VBR 13.1 on Windows needs a private fix for its Linux helper appliances, Virtual Labs and Azure helper appliances must be redeployed, recovery media made before the update must be recreated, and VBR 12.3 helper appliances and recovery media are still waiting for a private fix "soon". Kasten is not affected; customer-managed Linux repositories and proxies need distro kernels. No exploitation reported. Takeaway: check the History tab in Veeam Host Management to confirm the kernel update, redeploy long-lived helper appliances, and isolate 12.3 helper appliances until the fix lands, since backup infrastructure is a prime ransomware target.

VectorsNew attack vectors & techniques

  • WAF bypass via RFC 2231: ModSecurity 3.x before 3.0.17 ignores the filename* parameter that Go, Python (Werkzeug, Starlette), Node (busboy) and Spring parsers prefer, so an upload can carry one name past CRS filename rules and another to the app (CVE-2026-104269, CVSS 8.6 per GHSA; PHP backends unaffected). Fixed since late September, but HKCERT notes a public PoC today. Upgrade or normalize Content-Disposition upstream. GitHub advisory · HKCERT
  • Embedded-wallet single point of failure: the Keplr incident shows a social-login key service exposing private keys for many wallets at once, and the defender's "rescue" sweep using the attacker's own path was mistaken for theft by on-chain analysts. The Block · Bitcoin.com News
