Queens Public Library: ransomware knocks out Wi-Fi and digital services across 66 branches

The Queens Public Library in New York City, 66 branches serving about 2.3 million residents, confirmed a ransomware attack that encrypted systems and took down on-site computers, checkout and return machines, Wi-Fi, digital media services and the mobile app. Branches remain open; some programs may be hit. The system first flagged outages Wednesday afternoon and confirmed ransomware later that day. It says it is working with law enforcement and cybersecurity experts. No group had claimed responsibility as of Thursday evening, and no data theft has been confirmed. Takeaway: treat QPL-related phishing as high risk while recovery runs, and assume library-system playbooks remain a live ransomware target.

Fake OpenAI, SpaceX and Anthropic event pages chain Kali365 device-code phishing with VoidProxy AiTM

Push Security (5 Nov) found fake registration pages for an OpenAI Academy livestream, a private SpaceX AI briefing and Anthropic at AWS re:Invent that demand a work-account sign-in for AI rewards. Microsoft path: Kali365 device-code phishing, then a VoidProxy Microsoft AiTM page. Google path: VoidProxy reverse-proxy AiTM that steals credentials, MFA and session cookies. Push tracks the lure kits as ZoomMock and MSDRoot (MSDRoot from about 22 Oct); delivery uses Google and Zapier short links plus Cloudflare bot checks. Earlier related reporting came from ANY.RUN (Jul) and Cofense (29 Oct). Takeaway: treat AI-branded event invites that require Microsoft or Google sign-in as phishing until proven otherwise, and watch device-code flows.
