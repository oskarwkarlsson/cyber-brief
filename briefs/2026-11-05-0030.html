Crédit Mutuel Arkéa confirms attackers moved money out of its own account via a supplier

The French banking group says a "sophisticated attack" went through the platform of one of its service providers and let attackers make transfers from the bank's own account to other banks, from where the money was potentially converted to crypto. Arkéa would not confirm ZachXBT's estimate of more than $133 million but said it could "validate the order of magnitude". Customer accounts and assets were not affected, it says, and it disputes French reports that entry was via its Fortuneo subsidiary. The supplier is unnamed. Takeaway: a supplier-to-treasury path is the risk here; banks should review what third-party platforms can initiate payments from their own accounts.

Zyxel GS1900 switch zero-day was the real bug behind the Kapibala attacks on 996 devices

Zyxel has patched CVE-2026-107441, an unauthenticated out-of-bounds write in the web interface of ten GS1900 switch models that gives OS command execution. GreyNoise had blamed the Kapibala campaign (996 switches in 48 countries since about August 17, stealing configs and root hashes) on the June-patched CVE-2026-7273, but Binarly found the attackers' exploit worked against all firmware patched for that bug. Fixed firmware shipped October 22; the advisory came November 3. Takeaway: GS1900 owners who patched in June are still exposed; update now, keep management interfaces off the internet and rotate credentials.

Amadeus booking-display breach now hits JAL, Cathay, Korean, Asiana, Singapore and Malaysia Airlines

Six Asian airlines have reported breaches in the past week traced to a vulnerability in an Amadeus travel booking display environment. Japan Airlines says 92,000 customers were affected, Cathay Pacific 53,000 records and Singapore Airlines 46,000. Data includes booking numbers, names and flight details, and in some cases passports, frequent flyer numbers and emails. Cathay says the attackers used credentials assigned to a system account. Amadeus says no payment data was accessed and the flaw is fixed, but will not say how many customers are hit. Takeaway: expect more airline notifications and targeted travel phishing using real booking details.

CISA adds an old Apple pointer authentication bypass to KEV

CISA added CVE-2024-27834 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 4, with a federal deadline of November 18. The flaw, fixed by Apple in 2024 (iOS, iPadOS, macOS, Safari, tvOS and watchOS 17.5/14.5-era updates), lets an attacker who already has arbitrary read and write bypass pointer authentication, so it is typically one link in an exploit chain. CISA did not say who is exploiting it. Takeaway: any Apple device still on pre-2024.5 software is in scope; check fleets for stragglers.
