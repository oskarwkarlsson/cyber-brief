Iris Plaza: about 7.7 million customer records may have leaked from an external cloud service

Iris Plaza, the Sendai-based group company that runs household-goods maker Iris Ohyama's official online store, said in a 5 November notice that data kept on a third-party cloud service may have been viewed or taken. The store held records on about 7.7 million people: about 5.8 million members and former members (names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, gender, dates of birth and, for current members, encrypted passwords) and about 1.9 million non-members such as guest buyers and campaign applicants (names, addresses, phones and emails). Not every field applies to every person, and the company has not said which encryption it used for passwords. Credit-card data was not stored on the affected service, and Iris Plaza says its shops on outside marketplaces were not hit. It found a suspicious file on 28 October, shut the store and related sites that day, confirmed unauthorized access, and reported the case to police and Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on 29 October. As of 5 November it had no reports of misuse or financial loss; the actual number of records taken is still under investigation. BleepingComputer, in today's English write-up, says a spokesperson told them there was no direct contact from the attacker and the perpetrators have not been identified. Takeaway: anyone who used Iris Plaza should change reused passwords, watch for phishing that pretends to be the company, and wait for Iris Plaza's own email rather than clicking unexpected links.
