SYS010 abuses browser management

Bitdefender says a new version of the SYS01 infostealer, spread through hundreds of Meta ads for fake Wallpaper Engine and Lively Wallpapers downloads (one ad reached 6,275 EU users), enrols Chrome in an attacker-controlled Google admin console, sets Firefox policy, or enrols the Windows device in an attacker MDM for Edge, to force-install extensions users cannot remove. It repeats the UAC prompt every three seconds, up to 99 times, until the user approves. The extensions steal session cookies (bypassing passwords and 2FA) and read and can delete recent Gmail and Outlook mail; Meta business accounts are the main target. Takeaway: check chrome://management and browser policies on unmanaged PCs.

Remus "Gmail zero-day" is an extension

The Remus infostealer seller advertised from Nov 8 a "Gmail zero-day" that kept reading mail after password changes and session revocation. GenDigital and Invoke RE found no zero-day: a module drops a fake "Google Sheets" Chrome extension, works around Chrome 137+ blocking command-line extension loading by editing the profile and enabling Developer Mode, then pulls unread mail every minute from up to 10 signed-in accounts. Access survives password resets because the extension stays installed. Takeaway: look for unexpected Developer Mode or unknown extensions in chrome://extensions.

"GoodGuys" paid-interview phishing

Push Security has tracked since Oct 13 a campaign offering up to $600 for 45-minute "expert interviews" or webinars while impersonating Accenture, American Express, Coca-Cola, Culver's, Google Cloud, Nvidia, SpaceX and Spotify, sent via HireVue, SendGrid, Mandrill and Cvent. Fake Google and Okta sign-ins take corporate accounts only, and operators appear to relay whatever MFA challenge (SMS, codes, number matching, Okta Verify push) the real login shows. Push sees signs the kit was built with an AI coding assistant. Takeaway: brief staff that paid interview invites asking for a work login are phishing; phishing-resistant MFA blunts this.
